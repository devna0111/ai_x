Experience of economic and social factors and taste
The experience of taste is greatly influenced by the economic and social environment beyond the individual's taste response.,People make various food choices according to their economic conditions, and social class and cultural background are also important factors that constitute a gourmet experience.,In this chapter, we will delve into how economic and social factors shape the experience of taste.The Effects of Economic Factors on the Gourmet Experience
1.1 Income Levels and Food Choices
Income levels are an important factor in determining the range of foods an individual may choose.,High-income people can eat in restaurants with high-quality ingredients and sophisticated recipes, while low-income people often have to choose relatively inexpensive and accessible foods.,This goes beyond simple taste differences, and can also affect nutritional balance and health.1.2 Changes in Food Culture According to the Economic Environment
As the economy changes, so does the food culture in a particular region. In times of economic depression, food with cheap ingredients is popular, while when the economy is booming, it is likely that high-end cuisine and gourmet culture will spread.,For example, Japan's "genbinshoku" culture developed in a way that practiced a frugal diet in economic difficulties, which also affected the form of meals and the experience of taste.1.3 Foodservice Industry and Economic Structure
The food service industry is closely related to the country's economic structure; as the economy has developed, various food brands have emerged, and as globalization has progressed, an environment has been created where food from around the world is more accessible.,On the other hand, deepening economic instability has led many people to reduce eating out and increase the frequency of eating at home.2. how social factors shape the experience of taste
2.1 Differences in Gourmet Experiences among Classes
The social class has a large impact on an individual's gastronomic experience: in the wealthy class, they visit upscale and Michelin-starred restaurants and enjoy a special gastronomic experience, while in the low-income class, they often spend their time focusing on high-cost foods.,These differences also affect gourmet ideas and food preferences formed in certain classes.2.2 Food and Cultural Identity
Social background affects not only the experience of taste, but also the individual's taste in food; in certain cultures it is common to prefer spicy foods, while in others it is common to prefer relatively mild flavors.,For example, there is a strong spicy food culture in Korea, which is closely linked to historical and social backgrounds.2.3 Globalization and Change in Food Culture
In modern society, as globalization progresses, various foods are being combined and fused.,The spread of fusion cuisine is a new change in the traditional gourmet experience, which is affecting social and cultural identity beyond simple taste differences.,In addition, in a multicultural society, various food cultures coexist and have the effect of further expanding the experience of taste.Conclusion
Economic and social factors have a significant impact on the type, manner, and perception of the food that an individual experiences.,Various factors, such as income level, restaurant industry, social class, and cultural background, combine to form a gourmet experience, which goes beyond simple personal tastes and also affects the food culture of society as a whole.,Therefore, considering the economic and social environment in the process of understanding taste will help to form a deeper gourmet experience.
